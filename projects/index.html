Project title

Nasopharyngeal Carriage of Streptococcus pneumoniae Serotypes Among Children and Their Cohabiting Relatives in South India: A Cross-Sectional Study

Background and rationale

Nasopharyngeal carriage of Streptococcus pneumoniae is an important component of pneumococcal transmission within households and communities. Children and their cohabiting family members may contribute to the circulation of pneumococcal strains, including serotypes associated with disease. Understanding carriage patterns and serotype distribution can provide valuable epidemiological information for public health surveillance.

Project objectives

  • Determine the occurrence of pneumococcal carriage among children and their cohabiting relatives.
  • Characterize the distribution of pneumococcal serotypes in the study population.
  • Investigate patterns of carriage within households and explore relevant epidemiological associations.

Scientific significance

The study aims to improve understanding of pneumococcal carriage and household transmission dynamics in South India. Its findings can contribute to regional pneumococcal epidemiology and inform future surveillance and prevention strategies.

Project title

Development and Evaluation of an Economical Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification Combined with a Lateral Flow Assay for Rapid Detection of Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacteriaceae

Background and rationale

Carbapenem resistance among clinically important Gram-negative bacteria presents a major challenge for infection management. Conventional antimicrobial susceptibility testing and molecular methods can involve considerable infrastructure, time, and cost. Combining isothermal nucleic acid amplification with lateral flow detection offers a potential approach for simplifying the detection of selected resistance determinants.

Project objectives

  • Develop an economical LAMP-based approach for detecting selected carbapenem resistance-associated genetic targets.
  • Integrate amplification with lateral flow-based result visualization.
  • Evaluate analytical performance and diagnostic feasibility using appropriate reference methods and study specimens.

Scientific significance

The project explores a potentially accessible molecular diagnostic strategy for carbapenem resistance detection. The approach may help support timely laboratory decision-making, particularly in settings where rapid and cost-conscious diagnostic technologies are needed.

Project title

Development and Validation of a Multiplex Lateral Flow Assay for Simultaneous Detection of Common Bacterial Pathogens of Community-Acquired Pneumonia Among the Paediatric Population

Background and rationale

Community-acquired pneumonia is an important cause of illness among children, and identifying its bacterial aetiology can be challenging because several pathogens may produce overlapping clinical presentations. A diagnostic approach capable of detecting multiple bacterial targets in a single test could support more efficient pathogen identification.

Project objectives

  • Develop a multiplex lateral flow assay targeting selected bacterial pathogens associated with community-acquired pneumonia in children.
  • Establish an assay format that enables simultaneous detection of multiple targets.
  • Evaluate analytical performance and validate the assay using appropriate reference methods and clinical specimens, as specified in the study protocol.

Scientific significance

This project aims to advance rapid, accessible diagnostic technology for paediatric respiratory infections. A validated multiplex approach could support timely identification of bacterial pathogens and contribute to more informed clinical management and antimicrobial stewardship.

Project title

Development of Peptide-Based Agents for Endodontic Disinfection

Background and rationale

Persistent endodontic infections can involve complex bacterial communities and microorganisms that are difficult to eliminate completely using conventional disinfection approaches. Peptide-based antimicrobial agents are of interest as potential alternatives or complementary strategies for controlling infection-associated microorganisms.

Project objectives

  • Investigate selected peptides for antimicrobial activity against relevant endodontic pathogens.
  • Evaluate their potential for reducing bacterial persistence and controlling infection-associated microbial growth.
  • Assess their suitability for further development as endodontic disinfection agents using appropriate experimental models.

Scientific significance

The project explores peptide-based strategies to address persistent endodontic infections. The research may contribute to the development of new approaches for microbial control in endodontic treatment, subject to experimental validation and subsequent assessment of safety and efficacy.

Project title

Accelerated Diagnostics of Antibiotic Resistance in Gram-Negative Urinary Tract Infection Using Bioimaging Tools

Background and rationale

Urinary tract infections caused by Gram-negative bacteria are a significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenge, particularly when antimicrobial resistance limits treatment options. Approaches that accelerate the assessment of bacterial characteristics and antibiotic response may help address the time required for conventional diagnostic workflows.

Project objectives

  • Investigate bioimaging-based approaches for accelerating the assessment of antibiotic resistance in Gram-negative urinary tract infections.
  • Evaluate the potential of the selected imaging methods to characterize relevant bacterial responses or resistance-associated features.
  • Assess the diagnostic approach using appropriate microbiological reference methods and study specimens.

Scientific significance

The project aims to explore innovative diagnostic approaches for antimicrobial resistance in urinary tract infections. Its findings may contribute to the development of faster diagnostic workflows and support future efforts to improve antimicrobial treatment decisions.

Project title

Development of Monoclonal Antibody-Based Point-of-Care Test Targeting Burkholderia pseudomallei Membrane Proteins for Rapid Detection of Melioidosis

Background and rationale

Melioidosis is an infectious disease caused by Burkholderia pseudomallei. Timely diagnosis can be challenging because its clinical presentation may overlap with other infections, while conventional diagnostic workflows may require specialized laboratory resources. Antibody-based point-of-care technologies offer a potential route towards rapid detection of pathogen-associated targets.

Project objectives

  • Identify and develop suitable monoclonal antibodies against selected B. pseudomallei membrane-associated protein targets.
  • Develop an antibody-based point-of-care diagnostic format for detecting melioidosis-associated targets.
  • Evaluate analytical performance, target specificity, and diagnostic feasibility using appropriate reference methods and specimens.

Scientific significance

The project aims to translate pathogen-targeted antibody technology into a rapid diagnostic platform for melioidosis. Successful development and validation could contribute to improved access to timely diagnosis and support clinical decision-making in settings where melioidosis is an important public health concern.
